Missiles over Riyadh · A rift in North America

HOUTHIS STRIKE RIYADH

Three ballistic missiles are intercepted over the Saudi capital — the second attack on Riyadh in three days — as the war in Yemen spills across the Red Sea and the Houthis warn airlines and ports to stay away
The fighting, drawn schematically — not to scale. The four-year truce collapsed in July.

Yemen’s Houthis launched strikes on the Saudi capital on Saturday, hours after vowing revenge for a Saudi-led coalition “special operation” against the Red Sea port of Hodeida and other targets on Friday. An alert warned Riyadh was under a hostile aerial threat; two explosions were heard in the city.

The coalition said it intercepted and destroyed three Houthi ballistic missiles headed for the capital, and two more over the Jizan region. It was the second Houthi attack on Riyadh in three days: on Thursday a strike damaged the Public Investment Fund tower, the tallest skyscraper in the city.

The Houthis said coalition strikes had killed 41 people in the previous 24 hours, and warned airlines that Saudi airspace was “unsafe”, threatening Jeddah and every Red Sea port, the financial district and a power station at Al-Shuqaiq. Neither side’s casualty claims could be independently verified.

Washington · Halifax

PENTAGON CURBS DEFENCE TIES WITH CANADA

Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth has imposed new limits on military cooperation with Canada in a classified memo, the Washington Post reported on Friday; the Pentagon confirmed the move on Saturday. Intelligence sharing, NORAD and NATO work are untouched, officials say — but senators in Halifax called it a “slap in the face” to America’s oldest ally.

New York

TRUMP VOWS 10% CAP ON CREDIT-CARD RATES

President Trump said on Saturday he will work with the incoming Democratic Congress to cap credit-card interest at 10%, reviving a 2024 campaign promise. The median rate is about 22.2%; researchers say a cap would save Americans roughly $100bn a year in interest. Senator Warren answered: “Let’s cap credit card interest rates.”

The Hague

ROHINGYA HAIL GENOCIDE VERDICT; MYANMAR SCOFFS

Three days after the world court ruled 9–6 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya — the first time a state has been found guilty of the crime — survivors celebrated in the camps while the junta dismissed the verdict and China backed its objection. The court ordered reparations and a plan within two years.

FLORIDA DROWNS IN ITS WETTEST NOVEMBER EVER

Melbourne has had 22.59 inches of rain since 1 November — more than double its old November record — and the St Johns River has passed its 1953 crest. The National Guard was called out on Thursday as a second storm in three weeks flooded the Space Coast.
